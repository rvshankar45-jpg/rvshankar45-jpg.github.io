Every time an AI chatbot answers you, someone pays for it. It adds up fast, and it's a big reason many AI features never launch.

So I ran an experiment: how much of that cost can be designed away without making the answers worse?

I built a simple customer-support chatbot for a made-up plant shop and ran the same 140 questions through it twice: once the quick way, once the careful way.

What I found:

- The bill dropped from $1.95 to $0.69. That's 64% cheaper, with 66% fewer tokens. Quality dipped only slightly: 4.63 to 4.49 out of 5.
- The biggest win: give the AI only the relevant part of the manual, not all of it. That did half the work.
- Laya, a tool that sends easy questions to a cheaper AI, saved 12%. Trained on 270 examples, it saved 20%.

The surprise: giving the AI the whole manual, stored so it's cheap to reuse, gave the best answers of all, at 62% below the original cost.

Cheaper AI is mostly about good design, not cutting corners.
